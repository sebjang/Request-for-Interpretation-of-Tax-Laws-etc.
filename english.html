Request for Interpretation of Tax Laws · public edition · ROC-EV-2026-01 · V4.0 · 2 October 2026

Examination and evidentiary standards for
investor ledgers built from primary data

The public edition of a request filed with the Minister of Finance and Economy of the Republic of Korea. Applicant: Jang Sebong.

A U.S. fund determines, in its own closing of accounts and statutory tax computation, that part of a distribution was not paid out of earnings and profits and is therefore a return of the shareholder’s capital — a return of invested principal. It reports that on Form 8937 and in its year-end tax data, and the qualified intermediary refunds the tax withheld on that part. The determination itself, however, is not carried through to the ultimate non-resident investor or to that investor’s own tax authority, and the same amount is then included in the dividend-income tax base in the country of residence.

This request connects the issuer’s filings, the international withholding and reclassification records, the domestic financial-institution records and the investor’s own ledger to the same distribution events, and asks on what basis such a ledger and its supporting evidence must be examined, accepted or rejected under Articles 14 and 16 of the Framework Act on National Taxes.

New to this? Open the English edition first — 31 pages, the complete reasoning. The compiled file (251 pp.) then gives every exhibit behind it, each citation linked to the page it cites.

What is asked

This is not a request for a ruling on any individual taxpayer’s liability or on any particular assessment. It asks for general standards of legal interpretation to be applied to facts once they are established. It may be put in a single sentence:

Where, in an international securities transaction, the provisional characterisation of income at the time of first payment differs from the source information subsequently determined by the issuer’s closing of accounts and statutory tax computation and by the international withholding reclassification, by reference to which materials and to which stage of determination are the facts constituting the elements of taxation and the tax base to be established? And does that standard apply equally to the detection of omitted sources of taxation and to the correction of over-stated taxable income or tax base?

Twenty-one numbered questions follow that one, in the order in which the facts must be examined:

  • The status of a verifiable investor ledger, and the standard for finding facts that differ from it (questions 1–3)
  • The provision of Article 17 of the Income Tax Act to be applied and the identification of the elements of taxation; the scope of examination of international source data and the standards for accepting or rejecting conflicting records (questions 4–12)
  • Identification of the applicable subparagraph and the computation of “profits from a collective investment vehicle”; the status of the closing of accounts and the statutory tax computation; the reference by which a “reduction of capital” is determined; whether the question is one of the satisfaction of the elements of taxation or of an exception to taxation; and the stage at which the tax amount and the tax base are determined (questions 13–18)
  • What more is required for the elements of dividend income to be satisfied in respect of a return of invested principal, and whether the absence of a particular formal document may substitute for those elements (questions 19–20)
  • The standard where the provisional data and the subsequently determined data differ, and its symmetrical application (question 21)

Comparative material — seven currency areas

Exhibit C-12 reproduces the published wording of the tax authorities of the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Germany, Switzerland, Australia and Japan. It does not ask which country exempts a return of capital. It asks whether the elements of taxation for income of the current period are satisfied in respect of a return of invested principal and, if they are not, how the acquisition cost is reduced so as to carry the matter forward to later taxation — compared across five points: the determination of the source of the distribution; the assessment of income for the current period and its legal character; the adjustment of the acquisition cost; the position once the principal has been exhausted; and the place of information reporting. Foreign systems do not determine the classification of income in the Republic of Korea; this is comparative material for ascertaining the elements of taxation under Article 17 of the Income Tax Act, not a basis for applying domestic law.

What this material does not assert

  • The patent filings are not offered as legal authority or as proof of anything under tax law. They disclose the method so that the institutions concerned may independently verify and reproduce the results from the same primary data.
  • It is not asserted that a foreign tax characterisation determines the Korean classification of income.
  • No conclusion is drawn as to the income classification of any individual payment, any taxpayer’s liability, or the lawfulness of any assessment.
  • Where a break in the data lineage is shown (Volume II, exhibit R-02), neither its cause nor any resulting amount is asserted.

Orientation

The aim is to detect over-statement and omission with the same data and the same standard. Both directions are examined together: amounts within the acquisition cost that were recorded as income although they are a return of invested principal, and amounts that became taxable because the acquisition cost was exhausted or exceeded but were never carried through to the domestic tax records. This material serves neither side exclusively.
