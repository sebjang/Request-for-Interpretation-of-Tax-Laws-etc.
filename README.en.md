# Examination and Evidentiary Standards for Investor Ledgers Built from Primary International Securities Data

Public edition of a **Request for Interpretation of Tax Laws, etc.** · 30 September 2026 · Applicant: Jang Sebong

*(한국어 안내는 [README.md](README.md)를 보십시오.)*

This is the public edition of a Request for Interpretation of Tax Laws, etc. filed with the Ministry of
Finance and Economy of the Republic of Korea. It concerns the portion of distributions from U.S.-listed exchange-traded funds
that constitutes a **return of capital (a return of invested principal)**. The request connects
issuer filings, international withholding and reclassification records, domestic financial-institution
records and the investor's own ledger to the same distribution events, and asks on what basis such a
ledger and its supporting evidence must be examined, accepted or rejected under **Articles 14 and 16
of the Framework Act on National Taxes**.

## What is asked

This is not a request for a ruling on any individual taxpayer's tax liability or on any particular
assessment. It asks for **general standards of legal interpretation** to be applied to facts once they
are established. The request sets out twelve numbered questions:

- Whether a verifiable investor ledger may serve as the basis for examining and determining a tax base
  (questions 1–3)
- Which elements of the taxable event must be identified before Article 17 of the Income Tax Act is
  applied (questions 4, 7, 9)
- The scope of examination of international source data, and the standards for accepting or rejecting
  conflicting records (questions 5, 6, 10)
- How facts that cannot be verified domestically are to be established, and where the boundary of
  administrative authority lies (questions 11, 12)

## What this material does not assert

- The patent filings are **not offered as legal authority or as proof of anything under tax law.**
  They disclose the method and the computational steps so that a third party can reproduce or
  rebut the results from the same primary data.
- It is not asserted that a foreign tax characterisation determines the Korean classification of income.
- No conclusion is drawn as to the income classification of any individual payment, any taxpayer's
  tax liability, or the lawfulness of any assessment.
- Where a break in the data lineage is shown (Volume II, exhibit R-02), **neither its cause nor any
  resulting amount is asserted.**

## Orientation

The aim is to detect over-assessment and omission **with the same data and the same standard**. Both
directions are examined together: amounts within the acquisition cost that were recorded as income
although they are a return of invested principal, and amounts that became taxable because the
acquisition cost was exhausted or exceeded but were never carried through to the domestic tax records.
This material serves neither side exclusively.
