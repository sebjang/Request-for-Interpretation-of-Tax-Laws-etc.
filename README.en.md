# Examination and Evidentiary Standards for Investor Ledgers Built from Primary International Securities Data

Public edition of a **Request for Interpretation of Tax Laws, etc.** · 2 October 2026 · Applicant: Jang Sebong · Document reference ROC-EV-2026-01 · V4.0

*(한국어 안내는 [README.md](README.md)를 보십시오.)*

This is the public edition of a Request for Interpretation of Tax Laws, etc. filed with the Ministry of
Finance and Economy of the Republic of Korea. It concerns the portion of distributions from U.S.-listed exchange-traded funds
that constitutes a **return of capital (a return of invested principal)**. The request connects
issuer filings, international withholding and reclassification records, domestic financial-institution
records and the investor's own ledger to the same distribution events, and asks on what basis such a
ledger and its supporting evidence must be examined, accepted or rejected under **Articles 14 and 16
of the Framework Act on National Taxes**.

## What is asked

This is not a request for a ruling on any individual taxpayer's tax liability or on any particular
assessment. It asks for **general standards of legal interpretation** to be applied to facts once they
are established.

The request may be put in a single sentence:

> Where, in an international securities transaction, the provisional characterisation of income at the
> time of first payment differs from the source information subsequently determined by the issuer's
> closing of accounts and statutory tax computation and by the international withholding
> reclassification, **by reference to which materials and to which stage of determination** are the
> facts constituting the elements of taxation and the tax base to be established? And does that
> standard apply **equally to the detection of omitted sources of taxation and to the correction of
> over-stated taxable income or tax base**?

The request sets out twenty-one numbered questions:

- The status of a verifiable investor ledger, and the standard for finding facts that differ from it
  (questions 1–3)
- The provision of Article 17 of the Income Tax Act to be applied and the identification of the
  elements of taxation; the scope of examination of international source data and the standards for
  accepting or rejecting conflicting records (questions 4–12)
- Identification of the applicable subparagraph and the computation of "profits from a collective
  investment vehicle"; the status of the closing of accounts and the statutory tax computation; the
  reference by which a "reduction of capital" is determined; whether the question is one of the
  satisfaction of the elements of taxation or of an exception to taxation; and the stage at which the
  tax amount and the tax base are determined (questions 13–18)
- What more is required for the elements of dividend income to be satisfied in respect of a return of
  invested principal, and whether the absence of a particular formal document may substitute for those
  elements (questions 19–20)
- The standard where the provisional data and the subsequently determined data differ, and its
  symmetrical application (question 21)

## Comparative material — seven currency areas

Exhibit **C-12** reproduces the published wording of the tax authorities of seven currency areas: the
United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Germany, Switzerland, Australia and Japan. It does not ask
which country exempts a return of capital. It asks **whether the elements of taxation for income of
the current period are satisfied in respect of a return of invested principal and, if they are not,
how the acquisition cost is reduced so as to carry the matter forward to later taxation** — compared
across five points: the determination of the source of the distribution; the assessment of income for
the current period and its legal character; the adjustment of the acquisition cost; the position once
the principal has been exhausted; and the place of information reporting. Foreign systems do not
determine the classification of income in the Republic of Korea; this material is not a basis for
applying domestic law but comparative material for ascertaining the elements of taxation under
Article 17 of the Income Tax Act.

## What this material does not assert

- The patent filings are **not offered as legal authority or as proof of anything under tax law.**
  They disclose the data-processing method, the computational steps and the verification criteria so
  that the institutions concerned may independently verify and reproduce the results from the same
  primary data.
- It is not asserted that a foreign tax characterisation determines the Korean classification of income.
- No conclusion is drawn as to the income classification of any individual payment, any taxpayer's
  tax liability, or the lawfulness of any assessment.
- Where a break in the data lineage is shown (Volume II, exhibit R-02), **neither its cause nor any
  resulting amount is asserted.**
- The request records that, in an information-disclosure reply, performance-award material was
  answered as non-disclosable rather than as non-existent; **it does not assert that any amount was
  paid to anyone in any case.**

## Orientation

The aim is to detect over-statement and omission **with the same data and the same standard**. Both
directions are examined together: amounts within the acquisition cost that were recorded as income
although they are a return of invested principal, and amounts that became taxable because the
acquisition cost was exhausted or exceeded but were never carried through to the domestic tax records.
This material serves neither side exclusively.
